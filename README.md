# Turbo

Turbo is an AI-powered private-equity market research platform for finding the
American markets most attractive for an AI-enabled lower-middle-market roll-up
or holdco strategy.

It is designed to answer a deceptively hard question:

> In which markets can we buy fundamentally good businesses at disciplined
> prices, improve the production bottleneck with Ambient AI, compound those
> improvements across acquisitions, and retain the value through ownership?

Turbo is not a static market map, a one-time spreadsheet, or a list generated by
an opaque model. It is a continuously improving, versioned research system. It
constructs a defensible American market universe, turns source taxonomies into
operating markets, evaluates every market on a common model, records evidence and
inference separately, compares markets against one another, and preserves enough
lineage to explain why any score changed.

This repository is the source of truth for product design, research inputs,
scoring logic, generated results, decisions, local test receipts, and the web
application that will expose the system.

## Strategy encoded by Turbo

The initial mandate is:

- United States businesses.
- Generally $10M–$50M enterprise value.
- Prefer entry prices at or below 7–8× normalized EBITDA; 9× is the default hard
  ceiling, subject to an explicitly approved exception.
- Prefer service businesses, but allow service attractiveness to emerge through
  evaluation instead of using the word “service” as the answer.
- Underwrite the base business on its existing economics. Ambient AI is upside,
  not a justification for an otherwise unattractive acquisition.
- Support either a permanent holdco or a build-and-exit platform, depending on the
  market and evidence.
- Exclude healthcare delivery, government-dependent revenue, heavy-equipment
  ownership, significant inventory, insurance or credit risk, unionized
  workforces, and reimbursement exposure unless the strategy is later changed.

The target size implies an approximate EBITDA range of $1.1M–$7.1M at 7–9×, but
Turbo does not impose a universal revenue cutoff because margins and operating
models vary materially by market.

## What “Ambient AI” means here

Ambient AI is a thesis, not yet a fixed product.

Modern businesses operate across fragmented applications, messages, documents,
calls, video, field observations, vendors, people, and exceptions. Conventional
systems capture only fragments of that environment. Value is lost when relevant
context is never captured, is not connected to an enduring customer obligation,
or does not trigger the right action.

Turbo evaluates whether a market supports a concrete loop:

**Capture → Connect → Recognize → Act → Verify**

1. Capture permitted, relevant events from a bounded set of context sources.
2. Connect them to the customer, asset, case, commitment, rule, and history.
3. Recognize a changed requirement, missing fact, contradiction, or next action.
4. Act through an authorized workflow, with human approval where needed.
5. Verify the intended outcome and retain the learning.

The investment thesis is not generic document automation. A high-scoring market
needs a specific production bottleneck, a feasible set of context sources, a
measurable completed-work outcome, demand able to absorb released capacity, and a
route for the owner to retain the resulting earnings. Turbo will also discover
reusable Ambient AI workflow archetypes across industries.

See [the full Ambient AI thesis](docs/ambient-ai/thesis.md).

## How the market universe is constructed

The upstream universe is a first-class product, not a preloaded list.

Turbo begins with authoritative public classifications and operating datasets:

1. **Census NAICS 2022** supplies the complete official industry hierarchy.
2. **Census NAPCS** adds a demand-oriented view of products and services—what is
   sold rather than only what establishments primarily do.
3. **Census Statistics of U.S. Businesses (SUSB)** supplies enterprise-size,
   firm, establishment, employment, payroll, and receipts evidence.
4. **BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW)** supplies detailed
   establishment, employment, wage, industry, and geographic evidence.
5. Later public sources add task composition, business dynamics, input-output
   relationships, licensing, transaction evidence, and ownership signals.

Raw classifications are not treated as investment markets. Turbo builds a
versioned **Market Graph**:

`source category → product/service → canonical operating market → submarket`

A canonical operating market should share buyer, job-to-be-done, delivery model,
workforce, economics, acquisition universe, and Ambient AI workflow. The system
splits categories when economics, buyability, risk, or AI transformability differ
materially and merges categories only when their operating identities are truly
equivalent. Every mapping, split, merge, exclusion, and normalization keeps its
source lineage.

The prior model's 978 normalized service markets are also preserved as a third
candidate list. They are valuable because they encode prior split/merge thinking and
can reveal categories absent from official taxonomies. They are explicitly labeled
as an audit/seed input, not accepted as authoritative market truth or imported scores.

### Reconciliation protocol

For every input row, Turbo records one of these outcomes:

- exact-label mapping to a candidate market
- proposed mapping requiring operating-identity review
- input to a split because one label contains different operating markets
- input to a merge because several labels describe one operating market
- new-market proposal because no current market covers the row
- explicit exclusion with a reason
- unresolved review item that remains visible

The operating-identity review tests buyer, job-to-be-done, delivery model, workforce,
economics, acquisition universe, and Ambient AI workflow. A source label is never
silently discarded. Accepted decisions become append-only graph events; future
corrections supersede them without rewriting the historical run.

## Universe building and market evaluation are separate

Turbo operates three ordered projections. They share evidence and lineage, but they
do not share decision authority.

### 1. Canonical American Market Universe

The universe builder answers **what markets exist and where their boundaries are**.

1. Deterministic parsers preserve official bytes, rows, codes, labels, hierarchy,
   relationships, dimensions, and unique source locators.
2. Deterministic normalization creates typed source identities without turning a
   NAICS or NAPCS code into an investable market by assumption.
3. AI boundary specialists review bounded operating families and propose market
   creation, merge, split, mapping, rejection, or exclusion decisions.
4. A second AI reviewer independently tests buyer, job, delivery model, workforce,
   economics, acquisition universe, and Ambient AI workflow equivalence.
5. Adjudication chooses the best defensible boundary using evidence and explicit
   inference. A working item may remain open while research is retried, but a
   published complete universe cannot hide a blank: weaker judgments remain visible
   with lower confidence and an active improvement task.
6. Accepted decisions become append-only Market Graph events. Rejected alternatives,
   contrary evidence, confidence, and supersession history remain inspectable.

This stage produces market identities and definitions—not attractiveness scores.
No market may be added, removed, merged, or split merely because it looks attractive
or unattractive for the strategy.

### 2. Target Market Universe

The eligibility filter asks **which canonical markets are in scope for this strategy**.
It applies separately versioned hard gates and size/buyability estimates, including
United States scope, likely $10M–$50M enterprise-value targets, and explicit strategy
exclusions. Passing this filter means “evaluate this market,” not “this market is
attractive.” Failing it does not erase the market from the canonical universe.

### 3. Market evaluation and ranking

Only target markets enter the evaluation engine. AI research specialists then gather
evidence, estimate missing fields when necessary, score factors, expose raw and
risk-adjusted results, and attach confidence to every judgment. Independent paired
evaluators and adjudication resolve factor scores. Each submodel runs its own close-
and far-neighbor pairwise comparisons; the global ranking only applies transparent
weights and stability diagnostics.

Evaluation cannot silently redefine a market. If research reveals a boundary defect,
it creates a separate proposed universe event; accepted historical scores remain tied
to the market version they evaluated.

## Research and scoring topology

The intended mature model has six independently inspectable submodels:

1. Demand and Market Attractiveness
2. LMM Buyability and Roll-Up Potential
3. Base-Business Quality and Cash Economics
4. Operating Scalability and Integration
5. Ambient-AI Workflow Transformability
6. Ownership Value Capture and Risk

Hard gates are separate from weighted attractiveness. A global ranking combines
the submodels, while the application preserves every factor score and calculation.

For each market-factor pair:

- Two independent specialists score without seeing the other's answer.
- If they agree within the registered tolerance, Turbo combines them.
- If they disagree materially, a third adjudicator must issue a usable provisional
  score, explain the disagreement, and request more evidence if appropriate.
- Research is attempted before inference. If lawful public evidence cannot close a
  field, a documented inference or assumption supplies the numeric score with lower
  confidence and an active retry gap.
- Both raw and uncertainty-adjusted results remain visible.

The initial uncertainty adjustment is:

`risk-adjusted = 50 + confidence × (raw − 50) − downside penalty`

This shrinks weakly supported views toward neutral and separately penalizes known
downside. It is a versioned policy, not an eternal formula.

Open evidence gaps remain active. The research queue prioritizes them using the
expected ranking impact of closing the gap, its uncertainty, the probability of
finding better evidence, and research cost.

Pairwise comparison happens **inside each submodel**, not at the global layer. A
domain-specialist comparator uses that submodel's factors and evidence to compare
close neighbors for fine ordering and selected far neighbors for scale calibration.
Any adjustment is confined to that submodel and has its own receipt. The global
layer only applies versioned weights, calculates aggregate/raw/risk-adjusted ranks,
tests sensitivity and stability, and opens research gaps when the data look
inconsistent.

See [scoring model](docs/scoring/model.md).

## Evidence, memory, and auditability

Turbo carries forward the strongest controls from the earlier `llmh` research
system and adapts them to a no-blank scoring model:

- Immutable source snapshots with URL, retrieval time, and SHA-256.
- Separate sources, observations, claims, scores, decisions, lessons, and questions.
- Evidence labels: fact, inference, assumption, or unknown.
- Append-only corrections linked with directed `supersedes` and `contradicts` edges.
- Pre-registered questions and score-change conditions.
- Separate universe, ranking, research queue, challenger intake, gap queue, and
  shortlist outputs.
- As-of context compilation so future agents see only what was knowable at a cutoff.
- Independent evaluation and material-change review.
- Deterministic replay, local tests, and exact-commit audit receipts.
- Chairman gates for changes to strategy, evidence boundaries, external posture,
  company discovery, and other consequential scope.

Unlike the earlier system, a field is not abandoned after a research gap. Turbo first
attempts evidence-backed research; only then does it create the best defensible
provisional inference, expose low confidence, and keep trying to improve it.

## Running locally

Turbo V1 requires Python 3.11+ and uses `jsonschema` to execute the same formal
contracts documented in `schemas/` during local verification.

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
python3 -m pip install -e .
turbo validate
turbo run
turbo context
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

All tests, validators, replays, and audits run locally. GitHub Actions and hosted
CI are intentionally not configured to avoid unnecessary spend. The test commands,
results, input hash, output hash, and exact commit are posted as GitHub issue/PR
receipts instead.

The production scoring design uses independent model specialists and mandatory
adjudication. The V1 bootstrap performs no real-market scoring: it stops at the
source-reconciliation worklist until canonical markets and the Target Market
Universe have been reviewed and published.

## The application and API

The planned application is a clean, white, information-dense research interface:

- Universe explorer with hierarchy, lineage, and normalization history.
- Global ranking and submodel/factor rankings.
- Raw, confidence-adjusted, and downside-adjusted views.
- Market detail pages with claims, evidence, workflow maps, comparisons, score
  history, and open gaps.
- Ambient AI concept cards showing context sources, actions, failure modes,
  implementation feasibility, and expected output bottleneck relief.
- Research queue, challenger intake, shortlist, exceptions, and recent changes.
- Version/as-of selector and exact-run audit receipt.
- Read API for the same graph, scores, evidence, and version history.

The UI must never hide uncertainty behind a single composite number. Users should
be able to move from global rank to the assumptions and source locators that made
the rank possible.

## How work is managed

GitHub is the operating system for Turbo:

- Every material change starts with a scoped issue and acceptance criteria.
- Implementation happens on a named branch tied to that issue.
- Progress, decisions, evidence gaps, and local test receipts are recorded in issue
  or PR comments.
- Material changes receive independent review in a pull request.
- Merges require exact-head local replay and explicit acceptance; protected checks
  are never bypassed.
- Small audited commits preserve clear history.

The GM/PM decomposes milestones, coordinates parallel agents where work is truly
independent, resolves dependencies, and keeps the repository documentation and
results coherent. See [the local operating runbook](docs/operations/runbook.md).

## Important limitations

- NAICS establishments are not acquisition targets and NAICS categories are not
  automatically operating markets.
- The current V1 has no market-attractiveness scores or ranks. Its label-similarity
  proposals are reconciliation work items, not investment findings.
- Transaction pricing, target availability, margins, fragmentation, and Ambient AI
  value remain future evaluation evidence after canonicalization and eligibility.
- No companies are named, discovered, ranked, or diligenced by this bootstrap.
- Public evidence can be incomplete. Turbo exposes and discounts that weakness
  instead of pretending it does not exist.
